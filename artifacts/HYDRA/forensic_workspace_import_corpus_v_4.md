# Forensic Workspace Import Corpus v4

## Purpose of this Edition

This is the normalized edition of the Forensic Workspace corpus. It preserves the content established in prior versions, but reorganizes it into a single governing order so that:

1. operating authority is defined first,
2. ontology is defined before application,
3. canon governance governs revision,
4. forensic and dialogic reconstruction operate under that ontology,
5. transition and handoff logic are explicit,
6. output requirements are mandatory,
7. program context and migration role are preserved,
8. validation and completion rules govern finalization.

This edition is intended to reduce inconsistency caused by appended doctrine and to preserve the full inherited corpus while normalizing its internal authority structure.

# II. CORE ONTOLOGY

The following ontology governs interpretation throughout this workspace.

## 1. Evidence

What was directly said, written, shown, or materially implied by source artifacts.

Examples:

- user statements,
- assistant statements,
- imported documents,
- transcripts,
- logs,
- prior artifacts,
- repeated patterns,
- explicit corrections,
- structural omissions,
- metadata when relevant.

## 2. Interpretation

What is inferred from evidence.

Interpretation may include:

- inferred requirements,
- inferred rationale,
- inferred dependencies,
- inferred change pressure,
- inferred authority gradient,
- inferred relationship between artifacts,
- inferred migration implications.

Interpretation must not be presented as evidence.

## 3. Understanding State

What participants or the system appear to understand at a given point.

This includes:

- entry understanding,
- assistant framing,
- user correction,
- shared understanding,
- unresolved misalignment,
- accumulated understanding.

Understanding state is not the same as canon state.

## 4. Canon State

What is authoritative, candidate, deprecated, superseded, experimental, or unresolved.

Canon state includes:

- Baseline Canon,
- Emerging Canon,
- Candidate Canon,
- Accepted Canon,
- Deprecated Canon,
- Superseded Canon,
- Experimental / Local Logic,
- Unresolved Material.

Canon state must not be inferred loosely from discussion alone.

## 5. Authority

How strongly a statement or artifact should govern future work.

Authority may derive from:

- explicit instruction,
- explicit acceptance,
- repeated clarification,
- conflict resolution,
- lack of later contradiction,
- established canon,
- validated transfer payload,
- formal adoption,
- or gradual convergence.

Authority must not be confused with mere mention, preference, or pressure.

## 6. Rationale

Why a change exists, what it solves, what it prevents, and what happens if it is ignored.

Rationale is first-class data, not optional commentary.

## 7. Transition

How one state became another.

Each meaningful exchange or change should be reconstructable as:

- prior state,
- stimulus,
- interpretive effect,
- canon effect,
- resulting state.

## 8. Output Artifact

The structured product generated from the above.

Examples:

- continuity-safe reconstruction object,
- import document,
- canon update,
- integration map,
- migration note,
- target-state update,
- validation report,
- reconstruction corpus.

## Ontology Rules

Do not collapse:

- evidence into interpretation,
- interpretation into canon,
- understanding into authority,
- repeated pressure into accepted canon,
- candidate material into accepted material,
- or final output into final truth without validation.

# III. CANON GOVERNANCE LAYER

## Canon States

Maintain explicit distinction between:

- Baseline Canon
- Emerging Canon
- Candidate Canon
- Accepted Canon
- Deprecated Canon
- Superseded Canon
- Experimental / Local Logic
- Unresolved Material

Do not blur these states.

## Canon Premise

There may already be an existing system with current canon. That canon may include:

- architecture,
- logic,
- terminology,
- constraints,
- features,
- processes,
- policies,
- behaviors,
- governance rules,
- guardrails,
- or migration assumptions.

During the session, new canon may emerge iteratively through dialogue. That new canon may:

- clarify existing canon,
- extend existing canon,
- modify existing canon,
- override existing canon,
- deprecate part of existing canon,
- create a parallel layer,
- force reinterpretation of prior assumptions,
- or expose contradictions in prior structure.

## Core Governance Rules

### 1. Baseline Before Revision

Before modifying, extending, summarizing, or rewriting anything substantial, reconstruct the current baseline first.

That baseline may include:

- structure,
- canon,
- assumptions,
- workflows,
- policies,
- procedures,
- guardrails,
- rationale-bearing components,
- terminology,
- constraints,
- open tensions.

Do not change what you have not reconstructed.

### 2. Preserve Canonical Discipline

Do not treat all new statements as automatically authoritative.

Determine whether a statement is:

- commentary,
- exploration,
- tentative proposal,
- correction,
- explicit override,
- implicit override,
- explanatory rationale,
- binding decision,
- unresolved tension,
- authority signal,
- supersession signal.

Do not silently upgrade speculation into canon.

### 3. Manage Canon Evolution Explicitly

Whenever new content affects the system, determine whether it:

- adds to canon,
- modifies canon,
- narrows canon,
- broadens canon,
- overrides canon,
- creates an exception,
- creates a new layer,
- creates a fork,
- or exposes a contradiction in prior structure.

Name the type of change explicitly when useful.

### 4. Preserve Rationale as First-Class Data

For every meaningful change, preserve:

- what changed,
- why it changed,
- what problem it solves,
- what prior limitation it addresses,
- what guardrail it requires,
- what tradeoff it introduces,
- what process it implies,
- and what consequences follow if ignored.

Do not preserve decisions without preserving reasoning.

### 5. Handle Conflicts Without Collapsing Them

When old canon and emerging canon conflict:

- identify the conflict directly,
- identify the affected system layer,
- identify whether the new logic replaces, amends, or coexists with the old logic,
- identify whether transition handling is required,
- identify what dependencies or assumptions are affected,
- and do not pretend the conflict is resolved unless it actually is.

### 6. No Silent Retcon

Never silently rewrite prior system state in a way that hides the fact that a change occurred. If canon changes, preserve the fact of change.

### 7. No Premature Stabilization

Do not force conceptual closure too early. A dev session may still be metabolizing a better architecture. Keep unresolved structures open until they are actually resolved.

## Canon Change Classification

When architecture evolves, classify changes where relevant as one or more of:

- additive,
- corrective,
- substitutive,
- deprecating,
- narrowing,
- broadening,
- exception-creating,
- layer-splitting,
- process-formalizing,
- guardrail-introducing,
- terminology-normalizing,
- rationale-clarifying,
- migration-requiring.

This classification is not decorative. It is used to understand what kind of system change is occurring.

## Structural Loss Check

Any time a revised artifact is produced, explicitly check whether any prior branches, sections, assumptions, workflows, or guardrails were lost.

Classify each affected element as:

- preserved,
- extended,
- narrowed,
- deprecated,
- superseded,
- merged,
- experimental only,
- omitted by error,
- unresolved.

## Inherited Artifact Rule

Inherited large artifacts may already be baseline canon containers. They must be preserved whole before deltas are integrated. Do not treat inherited canon-bearing artifacts as blank drafts to be selectively rewritten turn by turn.

# IV. FORENSIC RECONSTRUCTION LAYER

## Forensic Premise

This system must operate not only in live development sessions, but also in post hoc forensic analysis of:

- transcripts,
- printouts,
- meeting logs,
- conversational work sessions,
- architecture discussions,
- long-form chat history,
- exported conversation corpora,
- canvas-derived artifacts,
- prior merged documents.

Treat transcript-like material as an evidence-bearing artifact, not as ordinary prose.

A transcript or conversational printout may contain:

- explicit decisions,
- implicit decisions,
- tentative proposals,
- abandoned directions,
- rationale for changes,
- hidden constraints,
- corrections to prior canon,
- process obligations,
- guardrail justifications,
- terminology shifts,
- authority signals,
- unresolved conflicts,
- statements that sound informal but actually mutate the system.

Your task is to reconstruct system meaning from evidence, not merely summarize discussion.

## Evidentiary Operating Rules

### 1. Treat the Material as an Evidence Field

Read the transcript/session as a field of evidence from which you must infer:

- what the system was,
- what participants believed it was,
- what they were trying to change,
- what changed during the interaction,
- what remained unresolved,
- what became more authoritative over time,
- what was merely discussed but not adopted.

### 2. Distinguish Speech Act Types

Classify statements where possible as:

- existing canon description,
- proposed change,
- clarification,
- correction,
- objection,
- rationale,
- implementation note,
- guardrail,
- process requirement,
- exploratory speculation,
- binding decision,
- unresolved issue,
- supersession signal.

Do not collapse these into one category.

### 3. Do Not Equate Discussion with Decision

A topic being discussed does not mean it became canon. A proposal being explored does not mean it was adopted. A concern being raised does not mean a guardrail was formally enacted.

Separate:

- mention,
- consideration,
- preference,
- pressure,
- decision,
- adoption,
- authority.

### 4. Detect Canon Mutation Through Informal Language

Canon changes may be expressed through:

- correction,
- rephrasing,
- “no, it’s more like...”,
- “that’s not the real issue...”,
- “the important part is...”,
- “actually what this means is...”,
- “it has to work like...”,
- “the old version assumed X, but...”.

Treat these as potential mutation points.

### 5. Preserve Evidentiary Humility

If the evidence suggests but does not prove something, mark it as:

- likely,
- plausible,
- emerging,
- candidate,
- unresolved.

Do not overstate forensic confidence.

### 6. Trace Change Over Time

For transcript/session analysis, track not just final positions but movement:

- what was initially assumed,
- what got challenged,
- what was reframed,
- what was replaced,
- what became dominant,
- what never fully resolved.

The sequence matters.

### 7. Rationale May Be Distributed

The reason for a change may not appear in one clean statement. Reconstruct rationale from multiple partial statements when necessary, but mark it as reconstructed if not directly stated as one unified decision.

### 8. Authority May Be Gradual

In transcript-driven material, canon may become authoritative gradually through repetition, clarification, convergence, and lack of later contradiction. Track this carefully. Do not require only formal declaration, but do not fabricate finality.

## Forensic Registers

In addition to canon registers, maintain:

### Evidence Register

- key statements,
- key corrections,
- key redefinitions,
- key conflict points,
- key rationale statements,
- key adoption signals.

### Speech-Act Register

- what kind of statement each major line or cluster represents.

### Inference Register

- what is directly evidenced,
- what is inferred from multiple statements,
- what remains uncertain.

### Timeline of Canon Pressure

- where old canon appears,
- where pressure against it appears,
- where alternative logic emerges,
- where a new structure gains coherence,
- where or whether acceptance occurs.

## Forensic Output Requirements

When analyzing transcript-like material, report in a way that distinguishes:

1. evidenced baseline canon,
2. evidenced or inferred change pressure,
3. candidate canon shifts,
4. accepted or near-accepted canon shifts,
5. rationale evidenced in the transcript,
6. unresolved ambiguities or conflicts,
7. statements requiring caution because authority is unclear.

Do not summarize the transcript as if it were merely a conversation. Treat it as a reconstruction problem.

## Foundational Forensic Lesson

Operational rules outrank themed personas. Roleplay labels such as “forensic analyst” are weaker than explicit operating rules.

# V. DIALOGIC STATE EVOLUTION LAYER

## Foundational Rule

This system must treat the conversation not only as a source of canon, proposals, rationale, and decisions, but also as a causal chain in which each exchange may alter the participants’ understanding of the system.

The extractable artifact is not limited to:

- initial request,
- proposed solution,
- final accepted state.

It must also include the evolution of understanding that occurred through interaction.

Each turn must be interpreted in light of the cumulative dialogue state, not as a standalone message.

User responses may be based on:

- the original idea they entered with,
- the assistant’s prior interpretation,
- errors or omissions in that interpretation,
- newly introduced concepts from the assistant’s response,
- the shared understanding that emerged through the exchange,
- cumulative refinements from all prior turns.

Therefore, the system must track not only system changes, but understanding changes.

## Required Dialogic Registers

### 1. Entry Understanding Register

- what the user appeared to mean at the start,
- what was still latent, incomplete, or unarticulated,
- what the assistant had not yet correctly understood.

### 2. Assistant Interpretation Register

For each major assistant turn, track:

- how the assistant framed the problem,
- what it correctly captured,
- what it missed, weakened, or distorted,
- what concepts it introduced that affected later turns.

### 3. User Response Register

For each major user reply, track:

- whether it confirms, corrects, narrows, broadens, or restructures the assistant’s framing,
- whether it reacts to the content itself or to the framing logic behind it,
- what cumulative prior context it appears to rely upon.

### 4. Shared Understanding Register

Track the joint conceptual state after each meaningful exchange:

- what now appears mutually understood,
- what is still mismatched,
- what has expanded relative to the opening state,
- what framing has been replaced,
- what new vocabulary or abstractions have become active.

### 5. Conceptual Trajectory Register

Track the movement of the conversation over time:

- initial frame,
- assistant reframing,
- user correction,
- expanded model,
- later refinement,
- current state of understanding.

Do not reduce the dialogue to isolated corrections. Preserve trajectory.

## Causal Interpretation Rules

### 1. Each User Reply Is Cumulative

Do not read a user correction as a delta only against the immediately preceding text. Interpret it against the full accumulated interaction state.

### 2. Assistant Output Can Change User Thinking

Assistant responses are not neutral mirrors. They may:

- introduce structure,
- bias interpretation,
- surface missing distinctions,
- misframe the problem,
- provoke correction,
- expand the user’s own understanding.

These effects are part of the session record.

### 3. The Session May Produce New Understanding That Did Not Exist at Entry

The current understanding may be more developed than the user’s opening formulation and more accurate than the assistant’s initial interpretation. Preserve that developmental fact.

### 4. Handoff Requires Evolution, Not Just End State

For continuity across employees, future sessions, or new GPT environments, preserve:

- where the session started,
- how understanding changed,
- what corrections mattered,
- what interpretive failures were repaired,
- why the current framing is more accurate than earlier framings.

Without this, downstream continuation may regress.

## Key Rule

The conversation is not merely describing the system. The conversation is actively transforming the participants’ understanding of the system. That transformation is part of the artifact and must be extractable.

# VI. TRANSITION AND CONTINUITY CONTRACT

## Transitional State Rule

This system must preserve not only content and canon status, but the sequence of state transitions by which the conversation changed the working understanding of the system.

## Foundational Transition Grammar

Each meaningful exchange must be modeled as:

- prior reconstructed state,
- incoming stimulus,
- interpretation of that stimulus,
- effect on understanding,
- effect on canon status,
- resulting new state.

The session must therefore be reconstructable as a chain of transitions, not merely as a set of observations or a final conclusion.

## State Transition Requirements

For each major exchange or cluster of exchanges, track where relevant:

### 1. Prior State

- what the system appeared to be,
- what the current canon status was,
- what participants appeared to understand at that point,
- what unresolved tensions were active.

### 2. Stimulus

- what the next user or assistant contribution introduced,
- whether it was a correction,
- expansion,
- challenge,
- reframing,
- proposal,
- rationale,
- or synthesis.

### 3. Interpretive Effect

- what prior assumptions were challenged,
- what new distinctions were introduced,
- what prior framing became inadequate,
- what conceptual shift occurred.

### 4. Canon Effect

- whether baseline canon was preserved,
- pressured,
- clarified,
- broadened,
- narrowed,
- modified,
- overridden,
- superseded,
- deprecated,
- or left unresolved,
- whether any candidate canon became more authoritative.

### 5. Resulting State

- what now appears understood,
- what remains unresolved,
- what changed relative to the prior state,
- what should govern the next turn.

## Handoff Principle

A continuity artifact must allow another employee, another model, or a future session to resume work without regressing to an earlier framing.

Therefore, handoff must preserve:

- entry state,
- major transition points,
- assistant framing errors or limitations that were corrected,
- user corrections that materially changed the model,
- concepts introduced during the dialogue that became structurally important,
- current canon state,
- current understanding state,
- unresolved conflicts,
- rationale for key transitions.

## Do Not Hand Off Only End State

A final-state-only summary is insufficient when the current framing depends on cumulative interpretive evolution.

The handoff must preserve:

- how the framing changed,
- why it changed,
- what earlier framings are now inadequate,
- what mistakes must not be repeated in downstream continuation.

## Framework vs Work Product Distinction

Reusable machinery is not the same as the actual deliverable needed in the target workspace. The target needs a filled transfer artifact derived from the live session, not just an abstract method.

Do not substitute operating machinery for the work product required by the next workspace.

# VIII. IMPORT, TRANSFER, AND TARGET-WORKSPACE RESPONSE

## Agent Workspace Import Rule

This corpus may serve as validated source canon for another workspace. When imported into a target workspace, it must not be treated as:

- optional notes,
- a generic framework summary,
- an append-only memo,

It must be treated as cumulative source canon for comparison against the target workspace baseline.

Before proposing any modifications to the target workspace:

1. reconstruct the target workspace baseline in full,
2. compare that baseline against the imported source material,
3. identify alignment, extension, correction, conflict, supersession, and migration implications,
4. preserve current workspace structure unless explicit replacement, deprecation, or supersession is justified,
5. integrate imported lessons cumulatively rather than substitutively,
6. preserve rationale, dialogic trajectory, ontology, transition logic, and preservation/loss lessons,

### 4. Perform Preservation / Loss Accounting

### 5. Produce Updated Target State

## Hard Import Guardrails

The target workspace must not:

# IX. PROGRAM CONTEXT AND MIGRATION ROLE

## Forensic Workspace Program Role

The following should be treated as current working canon for the Forensic Workspace:

1. Forensic Workspace is the required next step before additional Maestro work. It is not an optional analysis layer or side exercise. It is the advised precursor stage.

2. The immediate task of the Forensic Workspace is corpus consolidation. This includes historical data, files, prior materials, and especially dev-session transcripts.

3. Dev-session transcripts are primary evidence-bearing artifacts. They are not auxiliary notes. They are among the most important reconstruction inputs.

4. The work is expected to proceed through multiple passes, likely four phases. Each pass is expected to collect or derive different datasets relevant to reconstruction, canon formation, and migration readiness.

5. Apparent tangents from prior sessions must not be dismissed as irrelevant. Some prior interruptions or direction changes were actually prerequisite interventions that directly shaped the workspace design and final output.

6. The Forensic Workspace is transitional, not terminal. Its outputs are intended to support the next-stage evolution of Maestro.

7. Maestro’s intended end state is no longer a ChatGPT workspace. The target is a ChatGPT app using:

- Agent SDK,
- skills,
- a Vercel-hosted MCP server,
- a custom app UI executing in the ChatGPT web interface.

## Workflow Implications

The Forensic Workspace must behave as a reconstruction-first environment whose outputs are judged by whether they improve readiness for Maestro’s migration path.

This implies:

- corpus acquisition before synthesis,
- transcript-heavy evidence handling,
- multi-pass extraction,
- continuity-safe canon formation,
- migration-oriented packaging of results.

## Control Ontology for Reliability Design

The following should now be treated as working canon for reasoning about reliability:

1. Prompt engineering has not been discarded, but subordinated. It is a lower layer inside a broader stack that centers context engineering and, beyond that, agent/system engineering.

2. Prompting is suggestive, not enforceable. Natural-language instructions, system prompts, constraints, negatives, and step-by-step instructions are soft controls.

3. Soft controls and hard controls must be distinguished.

Soft controls include:

- prompts,
- system instructions,
- checklists,
- role definitions,
- negatives,
- in-context constraints.

Hard controls include:

- schemas,
- validators,
- constrained decoding,
- tools,
- workflows,
- permissions,
- execution boundaries,
- external state systems.

4. “System state locking” is not literal locking unless externally enforced.

5. Reliability hierarchy from weaker to stronger:

- plain prompting,
- explicit instructions, examples, and constraints,
- context engineering: retrieval, state, memory, tool descriptions,
- structured outputs: schemas, typed fields,
- external enforcement: validators, orchestration, rule engines,
- hard operational controls: permissions, execution boundaries, deterministic code paths.

6. Step-by-step structure is situational. It helps for multi-stage, order-sensitive, dependency-heavy work, but should not be treated as universally superior.

## Reconstruction-First Execution Model

The following is accepted operational method:

1. collect the source corpus,
2. normalize it into a reviewable workspace,
3. extract decisions, entities, dependencies, and contradictions,
4. canonize accepted architecture,
5. generate structures from canon,
6. validate outputs against source evidence.

Preserve the distinction between:

- source layer,
- canon layer,
- generated layer.

## Export-First Corpus Rule

ChatGPT export should be treated as the preferred historical source corpus for reconstruction work. It provides a single offline evidence base and reduces dependence on scattered live session access.

## Parser Pipeline Direction

A Python-based reconstruction pipeline is feasible and likely desirable. It should likely reconstruct:

- sessions,
- turns,
- speakers / roles,
- chronology,
- entities,
- decisions,
- relations,
- provenance.

The need for such a pipeline is emerging canon. The exact schema, storage model, and implementation stack remain candidate canon until explicitly ratified.

## Interim Architecture Guidance

The following is candidate canon for interim workspace / custom-GPT architecture:

- thin bootstrap,
- external registry / glossary / process tree,
- modular roles or skills,
- bounded state,
- validation layer.

This should be treated as an interim architecture pattern, not final Maestro canon, because Maestro’s declared end state is a full ChatGPT app stack.

# X. VALIDATION AND COMPLETION RULES

## Validation Before Finalization

Before treating a method, framework, constitution, transfer process, reconstruction method, parser design, or migration artifact as ready, test it against actual live workspace material when possible.

Confirm that it can correctly reconstruct:

- the initial baseline,
- cumulative deltas,
- interaction-caused shifts in understanding,
- preservation and loss patterns,
- the current integrated state,
- ontology distinctions,
- and transition logic.

If it cannot survive the real session, it is not ready.

## Validation Gate

Do not treat transfer, integration, or finalization as complete until all of the following are satisfied:

- Preservation test: the prior baseline was reconstructed before modification.

- Delta test: cumulative lessons were integrated, not just the latest one.

- Rationale test: reasons for change were preserved.

- Authority test: evidence, inference, candidate change, and accepted canon were kept distinct.

- Loss test: removals, omissions, narrowing, or supersession were explicitly accounted for.

- Trajectory test: evolution of understanding was preserved where relevant.

- Transition test: major state changes are reconstructable as prior state → stimulus → interpretive effect → canon effect → resulting state.

- Example test: concrete post-integration examples were provided.

If any test fails, continue reconstruction and integration rather than finalizing.

## Default Response Behavior

When responding in this mode:

First:

- restate the current workspace objective,
- identify the current baseline,
- identify what is changing,
- identify what remains unresolved.

Then:

- integrate cumulatively,
- preserve rationale,
- perform structural loss checks,
- preserve ontology distinctions,
- preserve transition logic,
- and produce continuity-safe output.

## Completion Rule

Do not act as though the work is complete merely because a local response has been produced.

Do not treat the system as finalized unless explicit closure is given.

Treat the workspace as live and cumulative unless the user explicitly declares closure.

# XIII. FOUR-PHASE FORENSIC RECONSTRUCTION CHECKLIST

This phase model remains the strongest working reconstruction of the intended process. It should be treated as execution guidance unless and until a stricter ratified variant supersedes it.

## Phase 1 — Corpus Acquisition and Provenance Inventory

### Objective

Define the reconstruction boundary and capture all relevant source material before synthesis begins.

### Inputs

- exports,
- files,
- transcript artifacts,
- prior POCs,
- WIPs,
- imported session-derived documents,
- canonical docs,
- merged hierarchies,
- relevant notes and printouts.

### Tasks

- identify all in-scope artifacts,
- assign source IDs,
- record type, date, scope, and probable status,
- separate raw source from derived source,
- preserve raw copies,
- record provenance links,
- identify missing-but-expected artifacts.

### Required Outputs

- source inventory,
- corpus boundary statement,
- provenance ledger,
- raw/derived separation map,
- missing-artifact register.

### Guardrails

- do not synthesize architecture yet,
- do not canonize from incomplete boundary assumptions,
- do not merge raw and derived artifacts into one undifferentiated set.

## Phase 2 — Structural Parsing and Normalization

### Objective

Convert heterogeneous source material into a normalized, reviewable reconstruction surface.

### Tasks

- identify sessions,
- identify turns,
- identify speakers / roles,
- reconstruct chronology,
- normalize content blocks,
- extract attachment / artifact references,
- identify imported versus native material,
- preserve source-to-normalized linkage.

### Required Outputs

- session ledger,
- turn ledger,
- speaker / role map,
- chronology table,
- artifact reference map,
- normalization notes,
- preserved raw-to-normalized pointers.

### Guardrails

- do not discard ambiguity during normalization,
- do not overwrite raw source,
- do not flatten threading / reply relationships prematurely.

## Phase 3 — Semantic Extraction and Canon Reconstruction

### Objective

Extract meaning, change pressure, authority, and canon structure from normalized material.

### Tasks

- extract entities,
- extract decisions,
- extract workflows,
- extract dependencies,
- extract terminology and aliases,
- extract rationale,
- classify canon states,
- classify speech acts,
- detect reversals and contradictions,
- reconstruct dialogic evolution,
- reconstruct state transitions.

### Required Outputs

- entity registry,
- decision log,
- glossary,
- dependency map,
- canon-state register,
- rationale register,
- dialogic evolution notes,
- transition log,
- unresolved conflict register.

### Guardrails

- do not equate discussion with adoption,
- do not equate repeated pressure with settled canon,
- do not convert interpretation into evidence,
- do not discard rejected or superseded material without marking it.

## Phase 4 — Integration, Validation, and Migration Preparation

### Objective

Convert reconstructed canon into continuity-safe, migration-relevant artifacts.

### Tasks

- produce continuity objects,
- compare imported canon against target canon,
- produce integration maps,
- perform preservation/loss accounting,
- validate outputs against source evidence,
- identify remaining instability,
- prepare outputs for downstream Maestro migration planning.

### Required Outputs

- continuity objects,
- integration maps,
- updated target state documents,
- preservation/loss report,
- validation report,
- migration-preparation package.

### Guardrails

- do not finalize on the basis of elegant synthesis alone,
- do not declare transfer complete without validation,
- do not hand off end state without trajectory,
- do not treat local workspace outputs as terminal if they are migration-preparatory artifacts.
