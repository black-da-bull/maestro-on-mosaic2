That's a great question, and it gets to the heart of what makes songwriting a unique craft. You've already identified several key elements, like story arcs, internal rhyme schemes, and authenticity, which are all crucial. But what truly separates songwriting from a rhyming short story is the presence of music and the way the lyrics interact with it.

A rhyming short story is primarily a literary work. Its rhythm comes from the words themselves—the meter and the flow of the language. The rhymes are there to create a specific poetic effect. The story's effectiveness is judged on its literary merit, such as character development, plot, and prose.

Songwriting, on the other hand, is a more collaborative art form where the words, melody, harmony, and rhythm all work together to create an emotional experience. Here's a breakdown of the key differentiators:

Rhythm and Meter (as they relate to music): While a rhyming story has a literary meter, a song has a musical meter. The lyrics must fit within the song's time signature and tempo. The stresses and syllables in the words need to align with the musical beats. A line that reads well on the page might sound awkward when sung if the natural emphasis of the words doesn't match the musical rhythm.

Melody: A song's melody is a powerful tool for conveying emotion. The lyrics are sung, not spoken, which means the melody can make a simple line sound joyful, mournful, or triumphant. The rise and fall of the melody can highlight certain words or phrases, giving them added significance.

Repetition and Structure: Songs often use repetition in a way that would be monotonous in a short story. The chorus, a key element of most songs, is designed to be repeated, driving home the main theme or idea. This structural repetition is a core part of a song's identity and is a way for listeners to connect with and remember the music.

Performance: A song is meant to be performed. The lyrics are not just read; they are delivered by a singer. The performer's vocal inflection, emotion, and stage presence all contribute to the overall impact of the song, adding another layer of meaning that isn't present in a written story.

The "Between the Rhymes": While strong rhymes are important, what happens between the rhymes is often more critical in a song. The goal is to write lyrics that feel natural and conversational, not forced just for the sake of a rhyme. A great songwriter will prioritize the message and the natural flow of the language, sometimes even using imperfect or "slant" rhymes to keep the lyrics from sounding too predictable or "cheesy."

In short, a rhyming short story is a literary work that stands on its own. A song's lyrics, however, are one part of a larger musical composition. Their effectiveness is intrinsically tied to how they work in harmony with the melody, rhythm, and overall performance.
